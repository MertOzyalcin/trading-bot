Section 01

Which Strategy for Which Stock?

Not every strategy works on every stock. The key is matching your strategy's logic to how that stock actually behaves. Here's a practical guide:

Strategy Best For Avoid When Your Bot Uses
RSI Mean Reversion
Stable stocks Range-bound
Stocks that go up and down in a predictable range. AAPL, MSFT, JPMorgan. These stocks "bounce" after dips. Strong bull trends (NVDA 2024). RSI never hits 30 on a rocket stock. v1, v2, v3, v4, v5
MACD Crossover
Trending stocks Momentum
Stocks with clear directional momentum. TSLA, NVDA, growth stocks. Catches the start of new trends. Sideways markets. MACD generates lots of false signals when price isn't moving. Backtest Lab
MA Crossover
Long-term Index funds
SPY, QQQ, large stable companies. The golden cross (50MA above 200MA) is one of the most time-tested signals in trading. Individual volatile stocks or short time periods — needs months of data to work. Backtest Lab
Bollinger Bands
Volatile stocks High swings
Stocks with big price swings. TSLA, AMZN, crypto-adjacent stocks. Buys at statistical lows. Slow-moving stocks or during trending markets — bands expand so much the signal loses meaning. Backtest Lab
💡
Rule of thumb: Stable dividend stocks → RSI. Fast growth stocks → MACD or Bollinger. Long-term index investing → MA Crossover. When unsure, backtest all four and compare the results.
Section 02

How to Read RSI

RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures how fast and how much a stock has moved recently. It always gives a number between 0 and 100.

RSI Scale — 0 to 100
OVERSOLD
BUY signal
NEUTRAL
No action
OVERBOUGHT
SELL signal
0 30 ← buy zone 50 — midpoint 70 → sell zone 100

What the numbers actually mean

📉
RSI below 30
Stock has been sold heavily. Could be a panic sell or bad news. Your bot sees this as a potential rebound opportunity.
😐
RSI 30 — 70
Normal territory. No extreme signal. Your bot waits patiently here. This is where the market spends most of its time.
📈
RSI above 70
Stock has been bought heavily and may be overvalued short-term. Your bot sees this as a potential pullback.
⚠️
Important: Oversold doesn't mean "guaranteed bounce." A stock can have RSI of 28 and keep falling. That's why your bot uses Claude AI as a second opinion before buying — RSI alone is not enough.
Section 03

How to Read Backtest Stats

After running a backtest you see six numbers. Here's what each one means and what a good value looks like:

📊
Strategy Return
Total percentage profit your strategy made over the test period. Always compare this to Buy & Hold — if B&H is higher, your strategy underperformed doing nothing.
🏦
Buy & Hold Return
What you'd make by simply buying on day 1 and holding until the end. This is your benchmark. Beating it consistently is harder than it sounds.
🔢
Total Trades
How many buy/sell cycles the strategy completed. Very few trades (0–2) means the signal rarely triggered. Too many trades means overtrading and high fees.
🎯
Win Rate
Percentage of trades that were profitable. Above 50% is good. Above 65% is strong. But even a 40% win rate can be profitable if wins are bigger than losses.
📉
Max Drawdown
The worst losing streak from peak to trough. If it shows -20%, your $10,000 dropped to $8,000 at the worst point. Below -25% is a serious warning sign.
⚖️
Sharpe Ratio
Return per unit of risk. Above 1.0 is good. Above 2.0 is excellent. Below 0.5 means the returns aren't worth the volatility you're taking on.
✅
Healthy backtest looks like: Strategy Return > Buy & Hold, Win Rate > 55%, Max Drawdown better than -20%, Sharpe Ratio above 1.0. If all four pass, it's worth testing live on paper first.
Section 04

Which Timeframe to Use?

The time period you test dramatically affects your results. A strategy that looks great over 1 month might be terrible over 2 years. Here's a guide to choosing wisely:

PeriodWhat It TestsBest Used ForWatch Out For
1M Very recent behavior only Checking if a strategy is working right now Way too short — any result is mostly luck
3M One quarter of the year Checking short-term momentum strategies Still too short for RSI or MA strategies
6M Half a year RSI and Bollinger strategies start to show real signal May miss seasonal patterns
1Y ⭐ Full calendar year The sweet spot for your strategies. Captures earnings cycles, seasonality, and market swings. Only one year — could be an unusually good or bad year
2Y Two full years More reliable signal. Spans multiple market conditions. MA Crossover needs this minimum to work properly
5Y Five years of data Long-term validation. If a strategy works here, it's robust. Results may be dominated by one big event (COVID crash, etc.)
💡
Best practice: Always test the same strategy across multiple periods. If it only beats Buy & Hold in 1Y but fails in 2Y and 5Y, the 1Y result was probably luck — not skill.
Section 05

Common Beginner Mistakes

These mistakes cost beginners the most money. Read every one — several of them are traps your bot can fall into too.

❌ Trusting one good backtest result
A strategy that returned +40% over 1 year on AAPL might have returned -15% on TSLA and -5% on MSFT the same year. One good result proves nothing.
→ Always test on multiple stocks and multiple time periods before trusting a strategy.
❌ Curve fitting (overfitting)
Tuning RSI thresholds until the backtest looks perfect on past data. RSI 23/67 that crushes 2024 data was built for 2024 — it won't work in 2025.
→ Stick to round numbers (30/70) and test out-of-sample (e.g. tune on 2023, test on 2024).
❌ Overtrading
Making too many trades based on weak signals. Every trade costs fees. 20 trades at 0.2% commission each = 4% eaten before profits.
→ Your bot's Claude AI filter helps with this — it rejects weak setups before you place an order.
❌ Going live too fast
Switching to real money before paper trading for at least 4–8 weeks. Paper trading feels fake but reveals real flaws in your strategy's timing.
→ Run your paper bot through at least one earnings season before touching real money.
❌ No stop-loss
Holding a losing position hoping it recovers. "It'll come back" has wiped out many accounts. Your v3+ bot has bracket orders — don't remove them.
→ Always have a stop-loss. Your bot's -5% stop means the worst case loss per trade is controlled.
❌ Risking too much per trade
Putting 50% of your account into one trade. One bad trade can cut your account in half. Your bot risks 2% per trade — that's the right approach.
→ The 2% rule means you can lose 50 trades in a row before going broke. That's real protection.
Section 06

When NOT to Trade

Knowing when to stay out is just as important as knowing when to trade. The best traders miss bad setups on purpose.

🔴 Earnings Week
Companies report quarterly earnings 4 times a year. Prices can move ±20% in minutes. RSI and MACD become meaningless next to a surprise earnings number. Check earnings calendars before placing any trade.
🔴 Fed Rate Announcements
When the US Federal Reserve announces interest rate decisions, the entire market can swing 2–3% in seconds. These happen roughly every 6 weeks. Avoid opening new positions the day of an announcement.
🔴 Major News Events
Wars, pandemics, banking crises. Technical indicators are useless when markets move on fear and headlines. When major news is breaking, your bot's signals mean nothing.
🔴 First & Last 30 Minutes
Market open (9:30–10:00 AM EST) and close (3:30–4:00 PM EST) are extremely volatile. Prices spike and crash without reason. Your bot runs at 9:31 AM — this is a known risk.
🔴 Stock Dropped >10% in One Day
A massive single-day drop might look like RSI = oversold. But it's probably bad news — a lawsuit, earnings miss, or scandal. RSI oversold + bad news = do not buy.
🔴 Overall Market in Freefall
If the S&P 500 (SPY) is down 5%+ in a week, individual stock signals are unreliable. Everything falls in a crash regardless of RSI. Consider pausing the bot during bear markets.
🚨
Most important rule: When you don't understand why a stock is moving, don't trade it. Uncertainty is not a trading signal — it's a reason to wait.
Section 07

Glossary — Every Term You Need

These are the terms you'll see in your dashboard, backtest results, and any trading resource you read.

RSI
Relative Strength Index. A number 0–100 measuring how overbought or oversold a stock is based on recent price changes.
MACD
Moving Average Convergence Divergence. Tracks the relationship between two moving averages to signal momentum changes.
Moving Average
The average closing price over N days. A 50-day MA is the average of the last 50 closing prices, updated daily.
Golden Cross
When the 50-day MA crosses above the 200-day MA. Historically a bullish signal for long-term uptrends.
Death Cross
When the 50-day MA crosses below the 200-day MA. A bearish signal suggesting a potential downtrend.
Bollinger Bands
Two lines drawn 2 standard deviations above and below a 20-day moving average. Prices outside the bands are statistically unusual.
Stop-Loss
An automatic sell order triggered if price falls to a set level. Limits your maximum loss on any single trade.
Take-Profit
An automatic sell order triggered if price rises to a set level. Locks in gains without manual intervention.
Bracket Order
One buy order paired with both a stop-loss and take-profit order. Your bot uses this so exits are automatic.
Drawdown
The percentage drop from a peak to a trough before recovery. A -20% max drawdown means your account fell 20% from its highest point.
Sharpe Ratio
Return divided by volatility. Tells you if returns are worth the risk taken. Higher is better. Above 1.0 is solid.
Paper Trading
Trading with simulated money to test a strategy without real financial risk. What your bot does right now on Alpaca.
Backtesting
Running a strategy on historical data to see how it would have performed. Does not guarantee future results.
Buy & Hold
Buying a stock and holding it indefinitely. The benchmark your strategy must beat to justify its complexity.
Market Order
Buy or sell immediately at whatever the current price is. Fast but you get whatever price the market gives you.
Limit Order
Buy or sell only at a specific price or better. More control but may not fill if price doesn't reach your level.
Oversold
A stock that has fallen so much it may be "cheap" relative to its recent history. RSI below 30 signals this.
Overbought
A stock that has risen so fast it may be "expensive" short-term. RSI above 70 signals this.
Volatility
How much a stock's price moves. High volatility = big swings. TSLA is high volatility. MSFT is relatively low.
Bull Market
A market trending upward over months or years. Most strategies work better in bull markets.
Bear Market
A market down 20%+ from recent highs. Most strategies struggle. Cash is sometimes the best position.
